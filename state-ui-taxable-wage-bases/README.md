# State unemployment insurance taxable wage bases and new employer tax rates by US state

The unemployment insurance taxable wage base for 48 US states and the District of Columbia — 49 of the 51 jurisdictions — plus the tax rate assigned to new employers where the state prints one. The wage base is the per-employee annual earnings cap on which unemployment insurance tax is owed. Nebraska and Rhode Island are not held: each sets two wage bases depending on the employer, so which figure applies is a condition rather than a number. Fourteen states are sourced from the state's own labor department or employment security agency, with the new employer rate where the state prints one. The other 35 carry the wage base only, read from the US Department of Labor's table 'Significant Provisions of State Unemployment Insurance Laws, Effective July 2026'; on those records the quote is the state's whole row of that table and the wage base is the last figure in it, under the column 'Wages Subject to Tax'. Answers 'what is the unemployment tax wage base in [state]', 'what UI rate do new employers pay in [state]', 'SUTA wage base by state 2026', and 'state unemployment insurance taxable wage base comparison'. The federal FUTA wage base is $7,000 and has not changed since 1983, but the jurisdictions held here range from $7,000 (California, several others) to over $78,000 (Washington), a 10× difference in the tax base for the same employee. New employer rates also vary widely. Changes take effect annually and states announce at different times (some January 1, some July 1, some fiscal year). Every figure carries the sentence or table row it was read from and a link to that page.
