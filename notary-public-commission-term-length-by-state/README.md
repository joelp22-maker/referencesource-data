# Notary public commission term length by US state — how many years a commission lasts before it must be renewed, quoted from the state's own statute

Every state commissions notaries public for a fixed term before renewal is required, but the length varies far more than most people expect — from 2 years (Delaware's initial term) and 3 years (Iowa) up to 10 years (Arkansas, South Carolina), with Ohio at 5 years except for attorneys, Michigan running 6 to 7 years on the notary's own birthday, and Louisiana alone commissioning notaries for life. A widely used free tracker (getlicensemap.com's 'Notary Renewal Requirements' chart) covers all 50 states plus DC for this exact fact, but its own sourcing note is a single blanket claim ('sourced from official state Secretary of State offices... and other state commissioning authorities') with no citation on any individual row — there is no way to check one state's figure against the statute it supposedly came from. This asset states the term length for each jurisdiction against the actual statute or administrative code section, quoted verbatim, so every row can be checked. Each record is one state; the term length is given exactly as the statute states it (a fixed number of years, a birthday-anchored range, or 'for life'), together with the citation. Answers 'how long does a notary commission last in my state', 'when do I need to renew my notary commission', 'is Louisiana the only state with a lifetime notary commission'.
