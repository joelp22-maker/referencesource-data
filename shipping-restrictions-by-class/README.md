# What can be mailed: USPS hazardous materials, plus item rules for post and couriers

Whether a named substance or item may be mailed, and on what conditions. Five records in six are the USPS hazardous materials table — Publication 52, Appendix A, 2,611 records — which is one row per Department of Transportation proper shipping name ('Ammonium metavanadate', 'Refrigerant gas R-404A', 'Diesel fuel'), carrying its hazard class, UN number, packing group, and whether it is prohibited or restricted in domestic mail by air and by surface. The remaining ~460 records are item-category rules of the kind a sender searches for by name: USPS's own consumer restriction pages, UK postal (Royal Mail, Post Office, Parcelforce, Royal Mail Wholesale), the UPU prohibited-articles entries for the US and UK, US CBP on the de minimis suspension, and six general duties from 49 CFR that bind every mode of transport and therefore bind the mail too. All three private couriers are covered as of 2026-09-01: FedEx (the International Connect prohibited and restricted item lists), UPS (the US Tariff/Terms and Conditions of Service — the items not accepted for transportation, plus the commodity sections on alcohol, marijuana and hemp, biological substances, firearms and ammunition, dry ice, limited quantity packages, hazardous waste and mercury, live animals, perishables, portable electronic devices, and tobacco and vaping), and DHL Express US (the prohibited commodities and the items needing prior approval, from DHL's own US 'what can I ship' page). Answers 'is UN2859 mailable', 'can I mail a lighter', 'USPS lithium battery mailing rules' and 'is [item] prohibited or restricted by [carrier]'. The carrier and scope fields on each record say whose rule it is and where it applies. For whether an item may be carried on an aircraft, which is a different question, see air-travel-item-restrictions — the 100 FAA, TSA, IATA and airline records that used to be in here moved there on 2026-10-07 and every one of their URLs still redirects.
