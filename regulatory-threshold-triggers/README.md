# US federal employment law thresholds by employee count

Employee-count thresholds that trigger US federal employment laws, with the number quoted from the statute itself or from the enforcing agency's own coverage page -- never from a vendor summary. Two kinds of record: a law that states a count (Title VII, ADA, ADEA, GINA, the Pregnancy Discrimination Act, FMLA, COBRA, the ACA employer mandate, WARN, EEO-1 reporting, IRCA) carries that number; a law whose coverage clause names who it covers without stating any employee count (FLSA, ERISA, the OSH Act, USERRA, FCRA, EPPA, the Equal Pay Act, the Consumer Credit Protection Act's garnishment rule, jury-duty protection) carries no threshold at all, is marked 'no employee-count threshold', and quotes the test its coverage does turn on. Note what the second kind does NOT say: that the law covers every employer. It says the statute sets no headcount -- several set some other test, and that test is quoted where the clause states one. A few laws state two counts, because a named class of employer is covered at a lower one -- EEO-1 reporting binds private employers at 100 but federal contractors at 50. Those records carry both numbers, so that reading only the headline figure cannot give the wrong answer to a contractor.
