# Final paycheck deadlines by US state — how long an employer has to pay a departing employee's last wages, quoted from the state's own labor law

Every state sets its own deadline for paying a departing employee's final wages, and most states set two different deadlines depending on whether the employee was fired (often immediate or within days) or quit voluntarily (often the next scheduled payday). Several states also set a specific penalty for a late final paycheck — California's waiting-time penalty continues the employee's daily wage for up to 30 days; Massachusetts mandates treble damages. Existing 50-state charts are maintained by payroll vendors as marketing content and, with one exception, do not cite or link the state's own statute per row — a comparison chart that cannot be checked against its own source. Each record is one state, with the deadline for an involuntary termination, the deadline for a voluntary quit where it differs, any stated penalty for late payment, and the statute or agency page it comes from, each backed by a verbatim quote. Answers 'how long does my employer have to give me my last paycheck in [state]' and 'what happens if my final paycheck is late'.
