# Every numeric requirement in OSHA's scaffold standard (29 CFR 1926.451) — dimensions, strengths, angles, safety factors and trigger heights

Every numeric requirement stated in 29 CFR 1926.451 (General requirements for scaffolds), extracted from the eCFR text and tabulated so a scaffold erector or safety inspector can look up any value without reading the full 60,000-character regulation. The values are not all dimensions: alongside the widths, gaps, clearances, overhangs, spacings and heights, the section states minimum strengths as forces in pounds, stairway and ramp angles in degrees, counts of parts (three wire rope clips, four rope wraps on a winding drum), wire-rope replacement thresholds in broken wires, load safety factors as multiples of the intended load, and the heights at which a duty such as fall protection or guardrailing begins. Each record is one requirement with its exact value as printed, in imperial and (where the regulation prints one) metric units, the paragraph citation, and the condition or scaffold type it applies to. Answers 'what is the maximum gap between scaffold planks', 'how far can a scaffold plank overhang its support', 'what is the minimum platform width for a scaffold', 'how much force must a scaffold toeboard withstand', 'how many wire rope clips does OSHA require on a suspension scaffold', 'what angle may a scaffold stairway be installed at', 'at what height-to-base ratio must a scaffold be tied', 'what is the load safety factor for scaffold suspension ropes', 'at what height does fall protection become required on a scaffold'. Scaffolding is among OSHA's top 10 most cited standards every year (6th in FY 2025) and the numeric values are scattered across paragraphs (a) through (h) of a long regulation -- a tabulation that puts them all in one place with their paragraph citations is genuinely useful and does not exist as a structured page anywhere found.
