# EPCRA Tier II hazardous chemical inventory reporting thresholds by state

The quantity threshold at which a facility must file an annual Tier II hazardous chemical inventory report, taken from each jurisdiction's own statute or rule and compared to the federal EPCRA Section 312 baseline. Federal thresholds: ≥10,000 lbs for hazardous chemicals, ≥500 lbs or the threshold planning quantity (whichever is lower) for extremely hazardous substances (40 CFR Part 370). EPA authorizes states to set lower thresholds and add chemicals. Seven jurisdictions are published here as doing so, each quoted from the instrument that sets it: California (55 gal / 500 lbs / 200 cu ft, Cal. Health & Safety Code § 25507), Vermont (100 lbs or the TPQ, whichever is less — the strictest general threshold found), New York City (500 lbs with a hazard-category table reaching down to 1 lb, 15 RCNY 41-04), Delaware (55 gal / 500 lbs, 16 Del. C. § 6306), Louisiana (500 lbs, LAC 33:V.10109), Oregon (a graduated table, OAR 837-085-0070), and New Jersey (EHS at 500 lbs or the SARA 302 TPQ, N.J.A.C. 7:1G). Sixteen states are published at the federal figures, each because its own SERC or environmental agency states those figures in its own words — Nevada, Utah, Virginia, Kansas, Maryland, Hawaii, Texas, North Carolina, Rhode Island, Illinois, Colorado, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Washington, Michigan and South Dakota. Four of them reach the same numbers through an instrument of their own: Illinois under 430 ILCS 100 and 29 Ill. Adm. Code 620, Pennsylvania under Act 165 of 1990 § 207(c), Ohio under ORC § 3750.08, and Hawaii which collects them on its own HCI/Tier II Form. Utah states the pattern outright — its DEQ answers the question directly: 'No, currently Utah has not implemented any other Tier 2 reporting requirements in addition to the federal requirements'. Nevada's International Fire Code quantities are a separate permit obligation that secondary sources routinely mistake for the reporting threshold. Answers 'does my California facility need to file Tier II for 600 pounds of a hazardous chemical' (yes — the California threshold is 500 lbs for solids, below the federal 10,000), 'what is Vermont's Tier II threshold', 'is Texas at the federal Tier II threshold', and 'which states have lower Tier II thresholds than federal'. States absent from this dataset are states whose own instrument has not been read, not states confirmed to match the federal baseline: a state appears here only once its own agency page states a number, never because no lower number was found.
