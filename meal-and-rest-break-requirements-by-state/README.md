# Meal and rest break requirements by US state — hours worked before a break is owed, its length, and whether it's paid, quoted from the state's own labor law

Most US states impose no meal- or rest-break requirement at all on private employers — federal law (the FLSA) doesn't require either. A minority of states do, and the rules vary sharply: the hours-worked threshold that triggers a meal break, its minimum length, whether it counts as paid time, and whether a separate rest break is owed on top of it, each set independently by that state's own statute or wage regulation. California requires a 30-minute meal break after 5 hours (a second after 10) and a paid 10-minute rest break per 4 hours; Illinois requires only a 20-minute meal break after 7.5 hours worked and no rest break at all; most states require neither. Free HR-vendor comparison charts on this topic (Paycor, HiveDesk, TimeClick) are comprehensive but cite no statute or regulation section, so a reader has no way to check a row against its source — and at least one such chart is already stale on Minnesota, whose meal- and rest-break law changed effective January 1, 2026. Each record is one state with a break requirement, giving the meal-break rule and the rest-break rule as the statute or regulation states them, each backed by a verbatim quote, plus the citation. States with no break requirement are omitted rather than asserted, since the absence itself has to be checked per state and isn't claimed here without a source stating it. Answers 'does my state require a lunch break', 'am I entitled to a paid break at work', 'how many hours can I work before I'm owed a break in [state]'.
