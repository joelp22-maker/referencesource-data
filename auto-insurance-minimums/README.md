# Minimum auto liability insurance limits by state

The minimum auto liability insurance a driver must carry in each US state and DC, as bodily injury per person, bodily injury per accident, and property damage per accident (the '25/50/10' shorthand), sourced one state at a time from that state's own statute, insurance department or DMV — never from an aggregator. Answers 'what is the minimum auto liability insurance required in [state]', 'minimum auto liability limits by state 2026', 'which states changed their minimum auto liability limits in 2025 or 2026 and what were the limits before and after', 'is it true that California's minimum auto liability limits are 15/30/5' (no — California raised its minimums for the first time since 1967, effective 1 January 2025, and NJ raised its minimums to 35/70/25 effective 1 January 2026), and 'what would I use to get state minimum liability limits as data rather than reading comparison sites'. No federal agency and no single publisher states this for all 50 states — it is 50 statutes and 50 insurance departments, five of which changed in the last two years (NJ, CA, NC, MA, UT, VA, HI all moved in 2025-2026), which is why every page-one search result is a lead-generation or law-firm site quoting numbers with no citation. A handful of states (Tennessee, California, New Jersey) also let a driver satisfy the requirement with a bond or cash deposit instead of a policy, at a stated dollar amount — recorded where the source states it. Florida and Michigan run materially different systems (Florida requires PIP/PDL, not a bodily-injury minimum; Michigan lets drivers choose down to a floor after a 2020 no-fault reform) and New Hampshire does not mandate insurance at all — these are recorded as stated, not forced into the standard three-number shape.
