# Hazardous waste generator categories — federal vs California vs Washington thresholds and obligations

The quantity thresholds that put a hazardous waste generator into a category, and what each category obliges, stated side by side for the three authorities where the answer diverges: federal EPA (40 CFR 262.13 generator category table), California DTSC (22 CCR 66262 — California never adopted the federal VSQG exemption, so a generator below 100 kg/month is still regulated at SQG level, with 180-day accumulation and manifesting), and Washington Ecology (WAC 173-303 — 'dangerous waste', where the state's word 'SQG' means what federal law calls a VSQG and the federal 'SQG' maps to the state's 'MQG'). The same kilograms put the same lab in three different compliance positions under three different names. Answers 'am I exempt as a very small quantity generator in California' (no — and the unaided answer's own framing is the trap), 'how much hazardous waste can I accumulate and for how long', and 'what does Washington call a small quantity generator'. Same fact from multiple authorities with disagreements stated — the juxtaposition class the strategy says is the strongest citation shape and the portfolio has almost none of.
