# Federal Hazardous Occupations Orders for Minors (29 CFR Part 570)

Federal child labor law bans workers under 18 from 17 numbered 'Hazardous Occupations Orders' (29 CFR 570.51-570.68) -- driving, roofing, mining, explosives, power-driven meat slicers, forklifts and other high-lift trucks, circular saws and more -- and separately restricts 14- and 15-year-olds to a closed, positively-defined list of permitted work (29 CFR 570.34), everything outside that list being prohibited (29 CFR 570.33). Limited apprentice and student-learner exemptions apply to some of the orders under 29 CFR 570.50. A state law stricter than federal law controls: North Carolina's own labor department bans nine further 'detrimental' occupations -- welding, work with a risk of falling 10 feet or more, electrician's-helper work, confined spaces, respirator use -- that federal law permits at 16. Each record carries the section it comes from and a verbatim quote of the regulation, so a parent or small-business owner asking 'can I let my 16-year-old operate a forklift' can check the answer against the rule itself. DOL's own summary at webapps.dol.gov/elaws lists the 17 orders one line each with none of the substantive detail; the state layer here is North Carolina only so far. North Carolina's Youth Employment Rules page states separately that 'Employment in hazardous or detrimental occupations are not permitted for youths under the age of 18', but the page listing the nine occupations does not repeat the age, so these records do not claim one.
