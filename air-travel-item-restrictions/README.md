# Flying with restricted items: FAA, TSA and IATA baggage and air cargo rules

Whether a named item may be carried on a US passenger flight, in which bag, and on what conditions — and for the lithium-battery rows, whether it may go as air cargo. 74 records are the passenger side: 64 from FAA PackSafe, one authority page per item ('Camp stove fuels', 'Duty-free perfume and cologne', 'Drones, unmanned aircraft systems (UAS)', 'Curling iron, cordless (butane-fueled)'), 4 more from the PackSafe printable chart for items with no page of their own, 2 from TSA's What Can I Bring, 2 stating what all US carriers require of spare batteries, and 3 from 49 CFR itself (175.10's passenger and crew exceptions, Special Provision A54, and the Hazardous Materials Table's 9A/9B aircraft quantity limits). The other 26 are the air cargo side: FAA's ICAO Technical Instructions lithium battery summary chart (13 records, PI 965 to PI 970 by section and watt-hour tier) and IATA's free Lithium Battery Guidance Document (13). Each record says, in its own fields, which authority states the rule and which bag or aircraft it applies to, because those are different statements: TSA decides what gets through the checkpoint, FAA decides what may be carried on the aircraft, and ICAO/IATA packing instructions govern freight. Answers 'can I fly with a power bank', 'can I take a lighter in my carry-on', 'are e-cigarettes allowed in checked baggage', 'dry ice carry-on limit', 'PI 965 Section IA cargo aircraft limit', and 'is bear spray allowed on a plane'. Where two authorities cover the same item the dataset carries both rows rather than picking one — spare lithium ion batteries appear three times, from FAA, from TSA and as the airline requirement — and where the editions genuinely diverge it says so: FAA's chart is built on the 2023-2024 Technical Instructions and prints no state-of-charge condition for PI 966, while IATA's 2026 edition makes 30% mandatory from 1 January 2026, so both are published and attributed.
