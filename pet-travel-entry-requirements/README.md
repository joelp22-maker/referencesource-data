# The dated, ordered steps for taking a dog, cat or ferret into the United States, the European Union or Great Britain — microchip, rabies vaccination, blood test and the waiting periods between them, quoted from the law itself

Getting a pet across a border is a chain of steps that only counts if it is done in the right order, and every link is a date. The microchip has to go in before the rabies vaccine or the vaccine does not count; the animal has to be old enough on the day it is vaccinated; then there is a wait before travel; and from an unlisted country there is a blood sample taken a set number of days after vaccination followed by a further wait of months. One step out of order costs three months and a plane ticket. This asset records each of those provisions as its own record, quoted verbatim from the instrument that states it — 42 CFR 71.51 for entry into the United States, Regulation (EU) No 576/2013 for the European Union, and the Defra guidance at GOV.UK for Great Britain. Answers 'what is the earliest my dog can fly?', 'does the microchip have to come before the rabies shot?', 'how long after the rabies blood test can we travel?', 'how old does a dog have to be to enter the US?', and 'when does the tapeworm treatment have to be given?'
