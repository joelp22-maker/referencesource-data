# Federal hours-of-service exemptions by commercial driver operation type

Commercial motor vehicle drivers are subject to federal hours-of-service limits (49 CFR 395.3: an 11-hour driving cap inside a 14-hour window, a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving, and a 60-hour/7-day or 70-hour/8-day on-duty cap), but 49 CFR 395.1 says the rules apply to every carrier and driver 'except as provided in paragraphs (b) through (x) of this section' -- a list of operation-specific carve-outs covering short-haul driving, agriculture and livestock, oilfield and well-drilling work, utility service, ready-mixed concrete, motion-picture production, railroad signal work, beekeeping, hi-rail and pipeline welding trucks, and more. Each carve-out removes a different requirement and carries its own radius, time window or condition, and they are scattered through one long section. Each record here is one exemption: who qualifies, the bright-line condition, and exactly which requirement it removes, quoted verbatim from the regulation. Answers 'do hours-of-service rules apply to my delivery route', 'is there an hours-of-service exemption for farm work', 'what counts as short-haul under the FMCSA rules'.
