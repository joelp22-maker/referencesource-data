# Nursing home minimum staffing requirements by US state — hours per resident day and RN coverage mandates after the 2026 federal repeal

What each US state requires of nursing homes (skilled nursing facilities) by way of minimum nursing staff, as the Consumer Voice 'State Nursing Home Staffing Standards Chart' (reviewed November 2021) states it. Each record is one state and gives the chart's own words for the direct care staff standard, the certified nurse aide standard where the chart has one, the licensed staff standard (RN and LPN/LVN) and the 'sufficient staff' standard; the chart's conversion of that standard into hours per resident day (hprd) for a 100-bed facility; and the citation the chart gives. The converted figures are Consumer Voice's arithmetic for one size of home, with the director of nursing counted at .06, and are not the state's rule: Arizona's rule is one nurse to 64 residents and its converted total is printed as 0 .54. An empty cell in the chart is an absent field here, never 'none'. Where the chart prints a standard for more than one year (Connecticut, Rhode Island) the record takes the latest and says which. New York and South Carolina are read from the state agency's own page instead, which is newer than the chart, and carry the hours those pages state. South Carolina's is a memo dated August 27, 2025 that sets its standard for one fiscal year (July 1 to June 30); the record carries the date and the period as the memo prints them. State law changes since November 2021 are not reflected for the other 49. The federal CMS minimum (3.48 HPRD + 24/7 RN) was REPEALED in December 2025, effective February 2026, and a moratorium through October 2034 was enacted via the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, so states are now the only authority. Answers 'what is the minimum nursing staff my state requires per resident' and 'is there still a federal nursing home staffing minimum'.
