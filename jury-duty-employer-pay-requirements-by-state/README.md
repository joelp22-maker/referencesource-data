# Jury duty employer pay requirements by US state — whether an employer must keep paying wages during jury service, how much, for how long, quoted from the state's own statute

Federal law (FLSA) does not require a private employer to pay an employee for time spent on jury duty — only eight states plus the District of Columbia impose any wage-continuation mandate at all, and where one exists the terms vary sharply: full regular wages with no cap (Alabama), full wages minus whatever fee the court itself pays the juror (Nebraska, Tennessee, DC), a flat dollar-per-day cap (Colorado $50/day, New York $72/day), or a mandate limited to a small fixed window (Louisiana covers one day only; Massachusetts and Colorado three days; Connecticut and DC five days). Several also exempt small employers below a headcount threshold (Tennessee under 5 employees; DC and New York's dollar floor apply only above 10 employees). Free HR-vendor comparison charts on this exact topic exist (Clockspot, Workforce.com) but Clockspot's own table flags five of its core-state rows — Louisiana, Massachusetts, Nebraska, Tennessee, DC — as 'commonly described, confirm before coding' rather than sourced to the statute text itself; this asset states all eight states plus DC against the primary code section. Each record is one jurisdiction with an employer pay mandate, giving the pay rule as the statute states it (amount, duration, any employer-size exemption) plus the citation. Jurisdictions with no mandate are omitted rather than asserted — the absence is the majority position and isn't claimed here without a source stating it for that specific state. Answers 'does my employer have to pay me for jury duty', 'how many days of jury duty must my employer pay for', 'can my employer deduct the court's juror fee from my pay'.
