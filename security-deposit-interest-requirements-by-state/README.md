# Security deposit interest requirements by US state — which states make a landlord pay interest on a held deposit, and at what rate, quoted from the statute

Most states let a landlord hold a security deposit with no interest owed at all. A minority require it — but the rate is rarely a flat number: Connecticut and Chicago reset their rate every year by regulator notice (Connecticut 0.49% for 2026, Chicago 0.01% for 2026), Massachusetts and New Hampshire peg it to the actual bank account or savings rate, and Minnesota is the one flat statutory rate (1% simple, non-compounded). Several also gate the duty on a condition a flat yes/no chart drops — Maryland only above a $50 deposit held 6+ months, New York only in buildings of six or more units, New Hampshire and Massachusetts only once the deposit has been held a full year. Illinois is two independent regimes, not one: the state law (765 ILCS 715) applies only to landlords owning 25 or more units and reads the largest in-state bank's passbook rate, while Chicago's own ordinance applies to any building inside city limits at a separately city-published rate — a landlord with five Chicago units owes interest under the city rule regardless of the state law's 25-unit floor. Each record carries the rate or rate-setting mechanism exactly as the statute or regulator prints it, any condition that gates the duty, and the citation, backed by a verbatim quote from the state's own statute or the regulator's own rate notice.
