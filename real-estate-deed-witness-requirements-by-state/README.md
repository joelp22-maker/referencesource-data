# Real estate deed witness requirements by US state — quoted from the state's own statute

A deed recorded without the witnesses its state requires can be rejected by the county recorder or, worse, held defective after the fact. Free notary-industry blogs repeat a consistent list of five witness states (Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, South Carolina) but none of the ones checked here cites the actual statute section, and they disagree with each other on the details: how many witnesses, and whether the notary can double as one. Georgia is the case they garble — its statute requires an attesting officer plus one other witness, and a second section says that officer may be a notary public, so the two blog answers ('two witnesses, notary cannot be one' and 'all require two except Georgia') are both wrong in different directions. This asset states each state's requirement in its own statute's words, quoted verbatim, together with the statute's own limits and alternatives — because in three of the five the witness rule is not absolute. Answers 'does a deed need witnesses', 'how many witnesses for a deed', 'can a notary be a witness on a deed'. These five states are the ones checked; the asset makes no claim about the other 45, which have not been read here.

Citation note, checked 2026-10-14: The Georgia row is cited to codes.findlaw.com rather than to an official state host, because Georgia does not publish its code anywhere a single section can be fetched at a stable URL — Georgia's own free O.C.G.A., which the General Assembly contracts LexisNexis to publish, is a JavaScript application at advance.lexis.com that serves no statutory text to a fetch.
