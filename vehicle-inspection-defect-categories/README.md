# Vehicle inspection defect categories: MOT major, minor and dangerous verdicts

What each vehicle defect is categorised as at a roadworthiness inspection, and therefore whether it fails the test. Each record is one defect under one authority, stating the defect reference, the defect wording as published, and the category (Minor, Major or Dangerous). Covers the DVSA MOT inspection manual for cars, vans and passenger vehicles (classes 3, 4, 5 and 7), the DVSA MOT inspection manual for motorcycles (classes 1 and 2), EU Directive 2014/45/EU Annex I, and the US minimum periodic inspection standards in 49 CFR Appendix A to Part 396. Answers 'is a cracked windscreen a major or minor MOT defect', 'MOT inspection manual zone A windscreen damage', 'does a worn wheel bearing fail an MOT', 'is a tyre bulge dangerous or major', and 'what fails an MOT test'. The same defect usually appears twice, once Major and once Dangerous, separated only by a severity qualifier, and the UK, EU and US verdicts for the same physical fault do not always agree. Only the DVSA manuals print the category as a word on the row. The EU directive marks it with an X under a column headed Minor, Major or Dangerous, so those records quote the table's header beside the category. Where one row is crossed in two or three columns for the two or three conditions it lists, the web page does not say which X belongs to which condition; the printed Official Journal (L 127, 29.4.2014) does, by the line each X is set on, and every such record here was checked against it on 2026-11-16. Two conditions of Annex I item 9.7 (a), 'Deteriorated condition.' and 'Damaged condition.', are left out: the print sets two X's beside the first and none beside the second, in English, French and German alike. Eight rows under Annex I item 3 (visibility: field of vision, glass, mirrors, wipers, washers) state a second condition on a table row of their own with a single X; the text of such a row cannot show which column the X is in, so it was read from the table's grid in the official XHTML and checked against the printed Journal, which sets each of those X's on its own line. Seven rows of the annex are not here: the three rows of minimum braking percentages under item 1.2.2, and items 6.1.3 (e), 8.2.1.2 (b) and 8.2.2.2 (a) and (b). The US appendix prints no category: every condition in it is listed under its opening line, which those records quote.
