# OSHA medical surveillance trigger thresholds by substance

The airborne exposure level that triggers mandatory medical surveillance under each OSHA substance-specific standard (29 CFR 1910 Subpart Z), compared across all regulated substances. Each record is one substance, carrying its action level, PEL, the exposure duration that triggers surveillance, the medical exam scope and frequency, biological monitoring requirements if any, and the record retention period. Answers 'what is the action level for lead', 'at what exposure must I provide medical exams for benzene workers', 'how often does cadmium biological monitoring need to happen', and 'which OSHA substances require 40-year record retention'. The action level is typically half the PEL but not always, and the surveillance trigger conditions vary wildly: lead uses blood lead levels, cadmium uses urine cadmium, and most others use airborne concentration plus days-per-year thresholds.
