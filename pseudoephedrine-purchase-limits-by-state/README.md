# Pseudoephedrine purchase limits: federal gram limits and stricter state rules

How much pseudoephedrine (Sudafed and equivalents) a person may buy, per day and per 30 days, quoted from the statute that sets each limit. Answers 'how much Sudafed can I buy in a month', 'is pseudoephedrine prescription-only in [state]', 'why was my Sudafed purchase blocked'. Federal law caps a buyer at 9 grams in any 30 days, of which no more than 7.5 grams may arrive by post or carrier (21 U.S.C. 844(a)), and caps a seller at 3.6 grams in a calendar day (21 CFR 1314.20). Several states are stricter: Tennessee 5.76 g/30 days and 28.8 g/year, Alabama and Illinois 7.5 g/30 days, Indiana, Kentucky, Mississippi and West Virginia 7.2 g/30 days, with annual caps in Indiana (61.2 g) and West Virginia (48 g). Others are stricter in kind rather than in grams — Arkansas, Mississippi, Oregon and Tennessee allow the sale only in a pharmacy. Oregon and Mississippi are the two states the open web still gets wrong: both are routinely described as prescription-only, and the statutes quoted here say in terms that the sale is lawful without a prescription. Coverage is the federal baseline plus the 11 states listed above; a state that is absent has not been checked, not been found to match the federal floor.
